Quantum circuits that help read the slide.

QuantumPath is a hybrid pipeline for oncology imaging. A classical CNN reads histopathology patches and CT slices; a small quantum layer makes the final call on a compact set of features.

How the hybrid pipeline works

Today's quantum computers have few, noisy qubits, so we never feed a whole image into them. The CNN does the heavy lifting and the quantum part sees only a short feature vector.

Why bother? Quantum feature maps can separate some small, high-dimensional datasets that are hard to separate classically, which matters in medicine where labelled scans are scarce. Whether it beats a good classical baseline is exactly what your benchmark should test.

Where it applies

Histopathology

Classify tissue patches as tumor or normal, or grade them. Whole slides are huge, so tile into patches and aggregate predictions.

Data: PatchCamelyon, LC25000, BreakHis

CT scans

Flag suspicious lung nodules as benign or malignant from cropped 2D or 3D regions around each candidate.

Data: LIDC-IDRI, MedMNIST NoduleMNIST3D

Oncology triage

Combine image features with tabular markers to prioritise cases for a specialist's review queue.

Data: Wisconsin Breast Cancer, TCGA subsets

Build plan for the hackathon

  1. Pick one dataset. MedMNIST (PathMNIST or BreastMNIST) is tiny and loads in one line.
  2. Train a classical baseline. A small CNN or ResNet18 gives you the number to beat.
  3. Extract and compress features. Use PCA or a linear layer to get 4 to 8 values.
  4. Add quantum models. Run QSVC, VQC and a QCNN on a simulator over the same features.
  5. Compare honestly. Report accuracy, AUC, sensitivity, training time and sample efficiency.
  6. Wrap in this site. Serve predictions from a small FastAPI endpoint.

Limits to state up front

Judges respond well to candour. Simulated qubits scale badly past about 20 to 25, real hardware adds noise, and gains over classical models on small data are often modest or absent. Medical use needs clinical validation, bias checks across patient groups, and regulatory approval. Present the system as decision support that a clinician reviews.
